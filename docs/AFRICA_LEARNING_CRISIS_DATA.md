## 🏛️ 2. Detailed Breakdown by Institutional Source

### **A. World Bank & UNESCO (The 89% Learning Poverty Benchmark)**
* **Definition**: Learning Poverty is defined as the inability of a child to read and understand a simple text by age 10.
* **Finding**: Sub-Saharan Africa has the highest learning poverty rate globally at **89%** (compared to a global average of 57% in low- and middle-income countries).
* **Impact**: Out of 150 million primary school-age children in the region, more than 130 million cannot read with comprehension.

### **B. UNESCO Global Education Monitoring (Language Mismatch)**
* **Finding**: 80% of children in Sub-Saharan Africa start primary school in a foreign/colonial language (English, French, Portuguese) that is not spoken at home.
* **Impact**: Creates immediate cognitive friction in STEM subjects. When children learn in their mother tongue (Kiswahili, Yoruba, Hausa, Zulu, etc.), comprehension increases by **30%**.

### **C. Usawa Agenda & Uwezo (East African Citizen-Led Learning Assessments)**
* **Kenya**: The *Foundational Literacy and Numeracy Assessment (FLANA)* report shows that 27% of Grade 7 learners entering Junior School cannot read a Grade 3 story, and nearly 3 in 10 fail basic Grade 3 arithmetic. In ASAL counties (Turkana, Garissa), the deficit exceeds 58%.
* **Uganda**: Uwezo assessments reveal over 25% of Primary 3 learners cannot read a single word of a Primary 2 story.
* **Tanzania**: Uwezo data demonstrates that over 60% of Class 7 primary school leavers fail basic English reading tests.

### **D. UNICEF & WHO (Special Needs & Inclusive Education)**
* **Finding**: Children with sensory or cognitive disabilities (blind, deaf, dyslexic) are 2.5x more likely to be out of school.
* **Impact**: Fewer than 2% of public schools have braille, tactile, or sign language teaching aids, and fewer than 1 in 10 teachers has special education training.
